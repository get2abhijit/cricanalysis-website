Cricket in Germany: a short, sourced guide

An old game with a new base. How the German federation came about, how the international team got T20I status, and why the numbers about German cricket are harder to pin down than they look.

By cricanalysis.in17 November 20268 min read

The short version: cricket in Germany is older than most people think and newer than it looks. Clubs existed in Berlin in the 19th century, but today's game was largely rebuilt after 1988, and in the last decade it has been driven by players who brought the game with them from South Asia and Afghanistan.

How to read this piece

Fact marks statements taken from a named source, listed at the end. View marks our interpretation. All sources were checked on 17 November 2026. Where sources disagree, we say so.

Disclosure: Abhijit Deshpande worked as a performance analyst for the Deutscher Cricket Bund from 2023 to 2025 and heads the cricket department of a club in Baden-Württemberg. This piece uses only public sources.

An early start, then a long gap

Fact Cricket was known in Germany from the late 18th century, and the first club, Berlin CC, was founded by English and American residents in the 1850s.[1] Before the First World War there was an active scene, especially in Berlin.[2]

Fact A first national body, also called the Deutscher Cricket Bund, was formed just before the First World War, representing clubs in cities including Berlin, Nuremberg, Fürth, Düsseldorf, Mannheim and Hamburg. Sources give the year as 1912 or 1913.[3][4] It did not last, and after the Second World War cricket was almost non-existent in Germany.[3]

The modern federation

Fact The current Deutscher Cricket Bund (DCB) was founded in 1988 in a document signed by representatives of eight clubs.[1] Interest had been revived by immigration in the 1960s and 1970s.[5]

Fact Germany played its first internationals against Denmark in 1989, according to two sources,[4][6] while the ICC's own history mentions a tour to Denmark in 1990.[1] The DCB joined the International Cricket Council as an Affiliate member in 1991 and became an Associate member in 1999.[1][2]

Becoming a T20 international side

Fact In 2018 the ICC decided to give full Twenty20 International (T20I) status to all its members. For men's teams, this applied to matches between members from 1 January 2019.[7]

Fact Germany's first T20Is were a three-match series against Belgium at the Royal Brussels Cricket Club in Waterloo on 11 and 12 May 2019, which Germany won 3–0. These were the first T20Is for both teams.[8]

View This change matters for analysts as much as for players. Since 2019, matches that used to be "unofficial" are T20Is, so they are recorded more consistently and many appear in free ball-by-ball datasets. For a country like Germany, most of its usable international data begins in 2019.

Data note

Germany's overall T20I win–loss record changes with every series, and free sources do not always agree on it. Check the current figure on an up-to-date source before quoting it; we do not repeat one here.

How the domestic game is organised

Fact Domestic competitions include a 50-over Bundesliga, a T20 Bundesliga, a women's Bundesliga (Frauen-Bundesliga) and youth leagues.[9] Below the national federation, regional associations run their own leagues. For example, the Baden-Württembergischer Cricket Verband (BWCV) covers Baden, Württemberg, Saarland and Rhineland-Palatinate, runs the Bundesliga, Regionalliga and Verbandsliga in its region, and says it has grown from seven member clubs in 2009 to 29.[10]

Fact German clubs also take part in the European Cricket League, a club competition across European nations.[5][9]

Who plays

Fact According to a DCB vice-president interviewed by Emerging Cricket (the interview refers to the 2024 season), the men's game is played mainly by people of Indian, Pakistani and Afghan heritage, with a smaller group from British, South African and Australian expatriate communities. On the women's side there are more German-born players from non-cricketing backgrounds.[9]

Fact The ICC notes that the arrival of refugees from cricket-playing countries in recent years led the DCB to spend much of its effort growing participation and infrastructure for them.[3]

How big is German cricket? It depends who you ask

This is where the numbers get slippery. Here is what the sources say:

SourceDateFigure
ICC member profile[2]end of 2016over 110 clubs, about 270 teams in competitive hard-ball cricket
Emerging Cricket, on the DCB five-year plan[11]May 2021almost 150 clubs across seven regions
Emerging Cricket, DCB vice-president[9]c. 2024around 7,000–8,000 participants playing a modified version of the sport (in addition to the main competitions)
DCB five-year plan target[11]set in 202115,000 people regularly participating

View These figures do not contradict each other so much as count different things: clubs versus teams, registered league players versus everyone who plays any form of the game. Club membership in Germany also changes quickly, because many players are students or on work contracts. When you see a single headline number for "cricketers in Germany", ask what was counted and when.

What this means for analysis

View Three things follow for anyone analysing German cricket:

  • Short records. Many players have played only a handful of T20Is, so their career numbers are fragile. See Five innings is not a career.
  • Uneven opposition. Germany plays a wide range of European opponents, from established sides to teams in their first seasons. Results need that context.
  • Thin player data. Basic facts such as batting hand and bowling style are often missing for Associate players, which is the gap our player profiles project works on.
