Who bats left, who bowls what

Free ball-by-ball data tells you who scored the runs and who took the wickets. It doesn't tell you whether a batter was left-handed or whether a bowler delivered left-arm spin. I'm slowly filling in those gaps.

By Abhijit DeshpandeNovember 20265 min read

First published on Abhijit's personal site; lightly edited for cricanalysis.in. Player counts are as shown in the demo.

Open the Player Profiles demo ↗

A scorecard without hands

Cricsheet is one of the best things to happen to cricket analysis: ball-by-ball data for thousands of matches, free, with every player given a stable ID. Much of what I do with cricket data starts there.

What it doesn't carry is how a player plays. The register has names and IDs, not batting hands or bowling styles. For an analyst, that's only half the picture. How does a left-hander fare against off-spin? Does a top order struggle against left-arm pace? You can't ask either question until every ball knows whose hand and whose style were involved.

Why it matters to me

I've been involved in cricket in different capacities over the years, but much of my more recent involvement has been in associate cricket: playing, coaching and analysis in Germany. The big names are easy; their profiles are everywhere. The opening batter from Austria or the spinner from Rwanda is a different story, and those are exactly the players I increasingly find myself looking at.

If the data doesn't know who bowls left-arm orthodox, the analysis starts with guesswork. I'd rather it started with the facts.

What it is

A player register that sits on top of Cricsheet. For each player in men's T20 internationals it records the batting hand, the bowling arm and style, and, where known, the playing role. Everything is keyed by the Cricsheet ID, so it lines up with the match data directly, and it can be downloaded as a spreadsheet.

FieldValues
Batting handRHB, LHB
Bowling stylePace: R/L + F, FM, MF, M, SM (e.g. RFM). Spin: OB, LB, LBG, SLA, LWS
Bowling statusbowls, does not bowl, not listed, unknown
Rolebatter, bowler, allrounder (batting / bowling), wicketkeeper-batter
Other IDsCricinfo, Cricbuzz, CricketArchive and others, so the data joins to other sources

The demo

The Cricket Player Profiles demo covers close to 3,900 players across more than a hundred teams, with an overview by team, the mix of bowling styles, a searchable player table, and a review queue that lists the players still missing information, most-capped first.

The dashboard code is on GitHub. The underlying data is kept separately, with restricted access. If you need access, please get in touch via the contact page.

Where it stands

Most of the well-known names are covered. Almost every player has a batting hand, and most have a bowling style recorded or are marked as non-bowlers. What's left is the long tail.

The remaining gaps are mostly associate players whose profiles don't exist anywhere online, or exist in one place under a different spelling. No script solves that. It takes someone who knows the player, or a scorecard read carefully. So I go through the review queue by hand, a few players at a time, and once I've entered something it isn't overwritten by the next automatic update.

Still plenty to do

  • Close the remaining gaps, team by team, starting with the teams I know best.
  • Playing roles: currently recorded for only about a third of the players.
  • Put it to work where it was always meant to go: matchup analysis, such as left-handers against off-spin, in future pieces on this site.

If you know a player whose batting hand or bowling style is missing or wrong, especially in associate cricket, I'd like to hear from you. LinkedIn is the easiest way to reach me.
